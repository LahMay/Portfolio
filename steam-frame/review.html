Review

Steam Frame is Valve's latest VR Headset, with advance specs and innovated features. Coming into two parts, being the headset and controllers, the headset is made of multiple parts. Those essential parts being the core module that holds the computer system that renders and process data, audio module that play out sound, head straps to keep hold, and various other parts.

The multiple components that make up the headset are well place like any other headset but what make it stand out a bit among other headset is customization. Steam Frame is capable of being modified, adding additional straps for better hold or holdings for those wearings glasses.

Despite the many components that made up Steam Frame, its weight isn't as heavy. Quite the opposite, being one of the lightest one out in the market, weighing but under 400 grams, distribute evenly for great comfort.

With a battery life of 48 hours, the controllers, like the headset, have many components and features. It has capacitive sensing for its input surface and grip for better finger tracking, two-stage actuating and gripping for personal control setting and lastly, uses magnetic tumbsticks for better reliability and smaller dead zone.

Working together, the headset and controllers have build in tracking that track each other. The headset have multiple camera on the surface, tracking the position of the controller their their IMU. Even if one position their control back like drawing a bow, the tracking system is build well that even place in a build spot, the tracking system can infer where the control is an adjust real time.

Steam Frame utilize a pancake-style lenses system. The reason for this style is because the pancake-style lenses system allow for higher fidelity compare to other style. This leads too a more immersive experience.

Taking one step further, Valve have improve upon some difficulties the pancake-style lenses system had. And that was that internal lighting, making it so that in bright areas, the internal lighting in the headset will be a bit glaring.

While in bright areas, one will still experience light glare, the level of intensity isn't as intense as before.

The size of the lenses that make up the headset is 2160x2160, using LCD panels and providing massive internal field of view. And the specs that work within are high. Without any setting changes, the headset works fine with 120Hz of graphic rending, lower to 70Hz for power saving while working as high as 144Hz.

Inside the lenses are Eye Tracking System that allow for one to display position of their eyes. Not only does this benefit social interaction as eye movement is a very much subtle part of communication, it also allow valve to implement the Foveated Streaming Feature.

The Foveated Streaming Feature is a feature where, by using the tracking data of the eye, they can adjust rending real life. Where one look, it is highly render while other parts outside will be blurry.

This feature not only allow for higher rending but also save on bit rate. Those saved bit rate matters a lot if your a streamer.

What this allow for is a good environment for streamers and other content creators as their recording will be much more efficient without the Foveated Streaming Feature.
